STEM Education in Australia: Pathways & Equity

An analytical exploration of secondary foundations, tertiary enrolment patterns, regional equity disparities, and future workforce readiness across Australia.

Section 1: School Level & Regional STEM Foundation

State & Territory Participation Choropleth

Senior secondary STEM engagement varies noticeably across state jurisdictions. While eastern states maintain STEM participation above 23%, regional territories like the Northern Territory lag at 10.5% due to resource constraints.

💡 Key Observation
State-level funding disparity creates an 11% participation gap between metropolitan hubs and remote territories.

Metropolitan vs Regional Gap

Specialized subject selection reveals severe rural barriers, with Metro participation exceeding Regional schools by over 10 percentage points in Physics and Advanced Math.

💡 Key Observation
Outer regional schools struggle to retain qualified STEM teaching staff, lowering advanced math enrolments.

NAPLAN Numeracy Proficiency

Foundational numeracy gaps broaden continuously from Year 3 to Year 9, with Remote proficiency falling below 20% compared to 65% in Major Cities.

💡 Key Observation
Early primary intervention before Year 5 is critical to prevent compounding numeracy deficits in secondary years.

State STEM Volume Dorling Cartogram

This Dorling cartogram distorts geographic boundaries into proportional circles scaled by total STEM enrolments. New South Wales and Victoria anchor the overwhelming majority of Australia's STEM cohort (exceeding 144,000 combined students).

Section 2: Higher Education University Enrolments & Field Distribution

Campus Enrolment Distribution

University-level STEM enrolments are heavily clustered around major capital campuses, with UNSW Sydney, Monash University, and The University of Sydney hosting the largest cohorts exceeding 15,000 STEM students each.

State STEM Enrolments & Gender Balance by Field

Use the dropdown menu below to inspect state-level enrolment volumes and gender disparities across key STEM fields. While Natural Sciences achieves gender parity (over 50% female share), Information Technology and Engineering display persistent gender gaps across all states.

💡 Key Observation
Female participation remains starkly suppressed under 23% in technical fields (IT & Engineering) across every Australian state, whereas life and physical sciences maintain equal representation.

Socioeconomic Disparity by Field

High SES domestic students maintain a significantly higher enrolment share across all fields of study, with the largest participation gaps appearing in Architecture, Creative Arts, and Health & Medicine.

Study Load Distribution

A comparison of full-time vs part-time study modes across states, reflecting different regional workforce integrations.

High School → Degree → STEM Pathways

Students originating from regional high schools transition overwhelmingly into Health and Agricultural sciences, whereas metropolitan graduates feed directly into Undergraduate Engineering and IT tracks.

Section 3: Academic & Discipline Breakdown

Domestic vs International Tile Map

International student concentration peaks in Victoria (38.0%) and ACT (37.2%), whereas Tasmania and Queensland display higher domestic proportions.

Discipline Enrolment Ranking

Computer Science and Software Engineering constitute the largest detailed sub-fields in IT, while Biological Sciences dominates Natural Sciences.

Graduate Performance Parallel Plot

Engineering and Computer Science graduates achieve high immediate employment (>86%) and top median starting salaries ($78,000–$82,000).

Section 4: Future Australian STEM Workforce & Projections

Projected Employment Streamgraph

Clean Energy and Tech/Cyber Security exhibit expanding streams toward 2030, driven by decarbonization and digital expansion.

10-Year Discipline Enrolment Trends

Computer Science and IT enrolments surged by 149% over the past decade, overtaking Engineering in 2022.
